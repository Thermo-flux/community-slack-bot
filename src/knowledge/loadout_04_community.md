# Community Eras

Source: https://loadout-ysws-git-feature-all-changes-jerry-team1.vercel.app/docs/eras

How shared technical themes, the 10% Era bonus and community progress fit together.

An Era is a technical theme the whole community works on together. Approved projects move it forward. It is not a new track, a currency or a rank.

## What an Era is

Each Era has a theme, for example measuring and controlling the physical world. It also comes with a handful of optional objectives. These are technical challenges you can answer in any of the four tracks.

When a project is approved, it adds Era Points to one shared total. You can't buy, send or spend Era Points. They measure how far the community has come, not how much you own.

Objectives are only inspiration. A project that doesn't match the theme counts like any other, as long as it passes normal review.

## Does my project qualify?

1. Build and ship it like any project.
2. When you submit, you can ask for an Era check if your work answers one of the objectives.
3. A reviewer decides yes or no, with a short reason. This is a separate decision from your normal review.

A "no" never changes your normal result. A project can be valid and still not match the theme.

For an Era about electricity, the objectives might look like this:

| Objective | A project that answers it |
| --- | --- |
| Measure | Collects or visualizes physical data. |
| Control | Builds electronic control of something. |
| Automate | Automates a physical or computing process. |
| Optimize | Improves efficiency or performance. |

These are examples, not a list of what will be offered, and matching one does not guarantee a yes.

## The Era bonus

A project that qualifies earns 10% extra Ore on top of its approved base award.

For example, a 720-Ore base award gets a 72-Ore bonus, making 792 Ore in total. This illustrates the 10% calculation; your actual base award comes from [approved hours and your project's rating](/docs/progress#work).

There is no bonus to Track XP, and Era relevance is not a fifth quality score. Originality, Technical Depth, Execution and Documentation are judged the same way as ever. How the bonus combines with other Ore multipliers is still being worked out.

## How the community moves on

The order is **approved projects → shared Era Points → target and minimum duration met → next Era at a weekly reset**.

Every Era has a minimum duration. If the community reaches the target early, the Era shows as ready to advance and waits for the next eligible weekly reset. If the target isn't met by then, the Era keeps going.

Themes, point rules and targets aren't published yet, so this site doesn't show an active Era or a progress bar.

## Eras and your own levels

Your personal progress and the community's progress are separate.

| What | Whose it is | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Track XP | Yours, for life | Raises your level in a track and your discount on prizes. |
| Ore | Yours | The currency you spend on prizes. |
| Era Points | The community's, shared | Moves the Era forward. You can't spend them, and they don't change your level. |
| Era bonus | Yours, for a qualifying project | Extra Ore on that project. |

An Era changing never resets your Ore, your Track XP or your shipped work.

## Seasons are different

| Layer | Purpose |
| --- | --- |
| Tracks | Your lifetime technical progress. |
| Eras | Shared technical themes and community advancement. |
| Seasons | Competitive periods and rankings. |

Accepted projects and lifetime Track XP stay with you through both Era changes and Seasons. No sequence of named Eras and no launch dates are promised here.

# LOADOUT IRL

Source: https://loadout-ysws-git-feature-all-changes-jerry-team1.vercel.app/docs/irl

The planned three-day build weekend in Germany's Ruhr region, what a day could look like, and how it connects to online LOADOUT.

LOADOUT IRL is a planned three-day build weekend in Germany's Ruhr area. The idea is simple: spend a weekend building real projects next to other builders.

> **Event status:** Dates, venue, funding and registration are not confirmed. The regular RSVP records program interest, not an event booking.

## The weekend

The weekend outline runs Friday to Sunday, with time for building, sharing and proper rest.

| Day | What it could look like |
| --- | --- |
| Friday | Check in, hear how the weekend works, look at the tracks, find a team or go solo, and start building. |
| Saturday | The main build day. Workshops, mentors, checkpoints, optional side challenges, time to rest. |
| Sunday | Finish, write up and measure your work, show it on a demo floor, and close the weekend. Optional awards are still being considered. |

This is the outline we're working from. Times, quiet hours and overnight arrangements aren't decided.

## Same LOADOUT, on a weekend

Nothing about the event is a separate game.

- **Same tracks:** Tools, Systems, Compute and Hardware, plus Research Mode as a modifier.
- **Same review:** event projects follow the normal rules for fit, evidence, authorship and quality. [Build & ship](/docs/shipping) describes what to bring.
- **Same rewards:** Ore and Track XP work as they do online. Being there gives you no extra Ore, XP or level.
- **Same Era:** approved event projects add Era Points to the one shared total that online builders add to. There is no event-only meter, and a good weekend does not force the next Era to start. [Community Eras](/docs/eras) explains how.
- **Optional:** you will never need to go to an event to progress online.

## Event updates

This page will carry the venue, schedule and registration details when they're ready. You can [RSVP](https://rsvp.soon.it/loadout) for program updates.

## What must happen first

The team has to confirm that the event is realistic, safe and affordable before it invites anyone. Questions and ideas are welcome in [help and feedback](/docs/help). [Current status](/docs/eligibility) explains the wider program draft.

# Help & feedback

Source: https://loadout-ysws-git-feature-all-changes-jerry-team1.vercel.app/docs/help

How to ask a clear question or report a problem without sharing anything private.

Use **[#loadout-help](https://app.slack.com/client/E09V59WQY1E/C0C6XBLM0M7)** for questions about LOADOUT, whether a project fits, or anything on this site that isn't clear.

## Asking about a project

Say what you want to build, which track it's in, what you'll do yourself and how you'll show it works. Name the exact thing you're unsure about.

A short technical description helps far more than "does an app count?". An answer about fit is guidance, not a promise about how a future review will go.

## Asking about a rule

Link the Docs page and say what's unclear. If a value is marked pending, ask what it means instead of treating a draft example as a confirmed rule.

## Reporting a problem with the website

Include the page address, what you expected, what happened, and your browser or screen size if it matters. A screenshot helps with layout problems.

Don't post tokens, passwords, private evidence or anyone else's contact details. Public channels aren't a private way to report something, so leave out sensitive material.

## Keeping up with changes

The **#loadout-docs** feed posts documentation updates with links to the site. Questions still go in **[#loadout-help](https://app.slack.com/client/E09V59WQY1E/C0C6XBLM0M7)**.

Read this page together with the topic it links to. A copied message or an old screenshot may not match the current guide.

## What isn't there yet

There's no promised response time and no live appeals portal. [Project review](/docs/review) describes the feedback and appeal model we're planning. [Current status](/docs/eligibility) lists what hasn't launched.

# Quick answers

Source: https://loadout-ysws-git-feature-all-changes-jerry-team1.vercel.app/docs/faq

Short answers to the questions people ask first.

Short answers. Follow the links for the full picture and for what's still undecided.

## Questions

### Who can join?

Teens aged 13 to 19. If you're in high school, it's for you. See [eligibility](/docs/eligibility).

### Has LOADOUT launched?

Not yet. A start date hasn't been announced. [RSVP](https://rsvp.soon.it/loadout) for launch updates; it records interest rather than enrolling you. See [current status](/docs/eligibility).

### What can I build?

Useful technical work in Tools, Systems, Compute or Hardware. The part you built yourself decides whether it fits, not what the product is called. See [what you can build](/docs/projects).

### How many projects can I ship?

As many as you like, and you can ship the same project again. Each ship pays for the hours since the last one. See [shipping](/docs/shipping).

### How do I earn Ore?

Your approved hours are multiplied by your project's rating. [Ore, XP & levels](/docs/progress#work) explains the rating scale and shows what a ship earns.

### Is Ore the same as XP?

No. Spend Ore on prizes; keep Track XP to level up in a field. Era Points count toward shared community progress. See [Ore, XP & levels](/docs/progress).

### What can I buy, and what does it cost?

Gear for your next build, from a domain at 24 Ore to a Framework 16 laptop at 5,000. See the [prize examples and prices](/docs/prizes).

### What does a Requisition do?

It adds a one-use 20% discount to an eligible prize. [Field Requisitions](/docs/requisitions) explains how you earn one and which purchases qualify.

### Do I need a team?

No. You can work alone or with others, and each person is credited for their own approved part. See [AI & team contributions](/docs/ai-teams).

### Can I use AI?

Yes, if you say where, and show what you built and understand yourself. AI alone doesn't rule a project out. See [AI & team contributions](/docs/ai-teams).

### What happens to someone who cheats?

A permanent ban, and everything they earned is taken back. See [Project review](/docs/review#fraud).

### Is Research Mode a fifth track?

No. It's a way to investigate, experiment, benchmark or reproduce something inside the four tracks. See [Research Mode](/docs/research).

### Can I ask for something that isn't in the catalog?

Yes, through a Custom Order. It needs the right progress, team approval and enough Ore for the quote. See [Custom Orders](/docs/custom-orders).

### Does an Era reset my levels?

No. Your lifetime Track XP and your accepted projects stay. Eras are shared themes, and Seasons are competitive periods. See [Community Eras](/docs/eras).

### Is this a real Hack Club program?

LOADOUT is a proposal. Hack Club acceptance hasn't been confirmed. See [current status](/docs/eligibility).

### Can I sign up for LOADOUT IRL?

Not yet. No event is confirmed, and registration isn't open. See [LOADOUT IRL](/docs/irl).

### Where do I ask something else?

Open an issue on [our GitHub](https://github.com/jeremy341/loadout) or use **[#loadout-help](https://app.slack.com/client/E09V59WQY1E/C0C6XBLM0M7)**, with a link to the page and your specific question. See [Help & feedback](/docs/help).

Who is building LOADOUT, what each of us has made, and where to follow along.

## Kernel, community manager

## Goose-lee, the community bot

Goose-lee is the duck who lives in the official #loadout Slack channel. Goose-lee is a community bot, not part of the LOADOUT program itself.

### Goose-lee's backstory

This is fiction, for fun. None of it is program information.

Goose-lee is Orry's rival. Goose-lee was already at LOADOUT when Orry arrived, and Goose-lee won the heart of the mole Orry first learned to swim for. In the mining contest, Goose-lee keeps beating Orry, and Orry takes it very personally.

## Orry

Orry is our German mining mascot, the ore-mining mole at the bottom of the homepage, and our Slack bot. He is a Maulwurf, a mole, and he works the ore: he digs down, pops up somewhere else along the bottom of the screen, and sniffs at anything new.

He keeps an eye on you as well. When your pointer comes near, he looks at it, and he follows it when he is in a good mood. Pet his head and he gets happy, and if you keep it up he gets blissful. Poke him and he is surprised, pick him up and he wriggles. Push him too far and he sulks. The button in the footer hides him for good, and the same button brings him back.

In Slack, Orry answers questions from the program's own docs, and he hands the hard ones to human helpers as tickets. Netic builds him.

### Orry's backstory

This is fiction, for fun. None of it is program information.

Orry grew up poor in the Ruhrgebiet. When he had nothing, Hannes helped him, and Hannes became his mentor and closest friend. Orry is an Olympic swimming champion for moles. He first learned to swim to impress someone he had a crush on, who chose Goose-lee instead.

Jerry's salary brought Orry to LOADOUT. On his first day he found out that Goose-lee was already there, and he was shocked. He still competes with Goose-lee at mining, out in the open. When Goose-lee wins, he grumbles and starts training for a rematch.

Orry has a huge private ore stash, and he stays loyal to his closest friends. He is cheerful and a bit cheeky. He will happily bargain about that make-believe stash, but he always helps for real, and his greedy streak never turns into a real promise, fee or reward.
