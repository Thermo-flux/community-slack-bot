# Ore, XP & levels

Source: https://loadout-ysws-git-feature-all-changes-jerry-team1.vercel.app/docs/progress

Ore is what you spend. Track XP is progress you keep. Here's how an hour turns into both.

[Program status →](/docs/eligibility#where-things-stand)

Approved work earns two things: Ore, which you spend, and Track XP, which you keep.

## Two kinds of progress

| What | What it does | Can you spend it? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ore | The currency for prizes. | Yes, once ordering opens. |
| Track XP | Your lasting level in Tools, Systems, Compute or Hardware. | No. |
| Era Points | The community's shared progress during an Era. | No. |

Getting more of one doesn't automatically give you more of the others.

## How an hour becomes Ore

**Ore = approved hours × your rating.** Other builders rate your project from 0 to 20, and the rating is how many Ore each hour pays.

| Rating | What it means | Ten hours pays |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 5 | Rough but honest: it exists, but it's unfinished or unclear. | 50 Ore |
| 10 | Solid: it works, it took real effort, and it's explained. | 100 Ore |
| 15 | Great: clearly above what most builders ship. | 150 Ore |
| 20 | Outstanding: the kind of thing you'd show an expert. | 200 Ore |

Twenty is the top of the scale. [Project review](/docs/review) explains what reviewers look at.

A project that wins the Era bonus (+10% on the approved Ore) adds it on top. There's no limit on how many hours or projects you can ship, as long as the hours are real.

## How an hour becomes Track XP

Reviewers assign Track XP to the fields your approved work demonstrates. Each project has one XP award, divided across those fields, so the same work doesn't receive a full award in several tracks. The exact equation and its quality weighting are among the [remaining decisions](/docs/eligibility#where-things-stand).

## Your field levels

Each track has fifteen levels, and you keep them for good. Level 15 takes about **350 approved hours of solid work in one track**. If you work in two areas, you reach level 15 in both after about 700 hours, so spreading out is fine. It just takes longer.

Levels **3, 6, 9, 12 and 15** each give you one [Requisition](/docs/requisitions). At LV.15, you have **3,500 Track XP and a 20% discount**. The thresholds and discounts between those milestones are still being set.

Levels don't multiply the Ore a project earns.

## What stays with you

Your lifetime Track XP, your accepted projects and your Digital Loadout carry over through Seasons and Era changes. Competitive ranks belong to their own period.

The idea is to build up real skill over many projects, not to spend your XP or start over each season.

## Where progress pays off

[Pricing](/docs/pricing) explains discounts. [Requisitions](/docs/requisitions) take another 20% off one prize. [Custom Orders](/docs/custom-orders) cover requests outside the regular catalog.

Most prizes are open to everyone, whichever track you work in. Your level in a related track takes up to 20% off, and a few specialist items may need a certain level.

[Pricing](/docs/pricing) shows how a price is worked out, with an example.

## Field Requisitions

A Requisition takes another 20% off one prize of 400 Ore or more. It isn't extra Ore and it isn't a coupon code you can pass on.

[The Requisition guide](/docs/requisitions) covers when you earn them, which track they match and the one-per-order rule.

## Custom Orders

A Custom Order is a request for equipment that isn't in the regular catalog. The team looks at what you need and, if it can support it, gives you a final Ore quote.

A request isn't an order. You need the right progress and enough Ore to accept an approved quote. [Custom Orders](/docs/custom-orders) explains the process.

## Availability

See [program status](/docs/eligibility#where-things-stand) for availability. The final quote must show the price and delivery conditions before you accept an order.

# Pricing

Source: https://loadout-ysws-git-feature-all-changes-jerry-team1.vercel.app/docs/pricing

How your levels and a Requisition reduce a prize's Ore price, with a worked example.

[Program status →](/docs/eligibility#where-things-stand)

Every prize has a price in Ore. Your progress in its track reduces that price. That doesn't change how much Ore a project earns.

## How a price is worked out

1. Every item has a **base price** in Ore and a track it relates to.
2. Your **level** in that track takes a percentage off the base price: up to 20% at level 15.
3. A matching [Requisition](/docs/requisitions) takes **another 20%** of the base price off one item.
4. You see the final price in Ore before you order, and you pay that.

Both discounts are a share of the **base price**, so they add up, and there's no cap in Ore. The most anyone can save on one item is 40%.

## An example

A prize with a base price of 1,000 Ore, bought at level 15 with a Requisition:

| Line | Ore |
| --- | --- |
| Base price | 1,000 |
| Level 15 discount (20%) | −200 |
| Requisition (20%) | −200 |
| **You pay** | **600** |

A Requisition only works on prizes of 400 Ore or more, so small prizes just get your level discount.

## When an item relates to two tracks

You get the better of your two discounts, not both added together. That's how focusing on one track pays off: it takes about 350 hours to reach 20% in one track, and about twice that to reach it in two.

## Buying outside your specialty

Most prizes are open to everyone, whichever track you work in. A discount can't unlock equipment that has its own level requirement, and a Requisition can't skip that requirement either.

## Still to be confirmed

The prices on the [prize list](/docs/prizes) are the ones we're planning, not final. How tax and delivery are handled isn't decided. For something outside the catalog, the [Custom Order process](/docs/custom-orders) uses the final approved quote, not your first estimate.

# Field Requisitions

Source: https://loadout-ysws-git-feature-all-changes-jerry-team1.vercel.app/docs/requisitions

A one-use ticket that takes another 20% off one prize, without skipping any access rules.

[Program status →](/docs/eligibility#where-things-stand)

A Field Requisition is a ticket that takes another 20% off one prize. It isn't another currency.

## How you earn one

You earn one Requisition each time you reach levels **3, 6, 9, 12 and 15** in a track. That's five per track, and they're all the same. [Ore, XP & levels](/docs/progress) explains how your work builds track progress.

## What one does

It takes **20% of the base price** off one prize. That comes on top of your level discount, which is also a share of the base price, so the two add up to at most 40%.

| Line | A 1,000 Ore prize |
| --- | --- |
| Level 15 discount (20%) | −200 |
| Requisition (20%) | −200 |
| **You pay** | **600 Ore** |

There's no limit in Ore on how much a Requisition saves. It only works on prizes of **400 Ore or more**, so you can't spend one on something small.

## Using one

The steps are: **pick a prize, check that you have a Requisition for its track, look at the final price, confirm the order.**

The prize has to relate to the Requisition's track. For a Custom Order, the approved quote has to say it's Requisition-eligible.

## The limits

- One use. It can't be transferred, and it never expires.
- Only one Requisition on an order.
- It doesn't add Ore, and it isn't a permanent discount.
- It doesn't remove a level requirement.
- It doesn't make an impossible request possible.
- If the program cancels an order, you get the Requisition back.

We may add a few rare General Requisitions later for exceptional contributions to the community. Whether we will, and how they'd be awarded, isn't decided.

## Not a coupon

You don't get a public code to share. A Requisition is a ticket you earn by building, and you spend it on one prize.

[Pricing](/docs/pricing) shows a full example, and [Custom Orders](/docs/custom-orders) explains requests.

# Custom Orders

Source: https://loadout-ysws-git-feature-all-changes-jerry-team1.vercel.app/docs/custom-orders

How to request equipment outside the shop, what the team checks, and what a quote includes.

[Program status →](/docs/eligibility#where-things-stand)

Want equipment that isn't in the shop? A Custom Order lets you ask for it. Explain what you need and what you'll use it for, and the team checks whether it can source, fund and deliver it.

## What you need to ask

You need **approved work, the right track level or request tier, and enough Ore for the final quote**.

Shipped, reviewed work builds Track XP and raises your level. [Ore, XP & levels](/docs/progress#levels) explains that progression. The level required for each kind of Custom Order is still being decided.

A Requisition can't get you past a level requirement.

## From request to quote

1. Explain the item, what it's for and how it relates to your projects.
2. Add an item or supplier link and any specs that matter.
3. The team checks that it fits the track, the budget, whether it's available and whether it can be delivered.
4. If it can support it, the team sends you the final Ore quote and any conditions.
5. You accept the quote, if you have enough Ore, or you decline it.

A request isn't a purchase. Wait for the team's quote before treating a price as agreed.

## Example: a separate machine for your home server

You've built a home server and want a small, low-power PC so it can run without tying up your everyday computer. The model you want isn't in the shop.

Here's what you would include:

| Part of the request | What you would send |
| --- | --- |
| Item | A supplier link, the model, and the RAM, storage and ports you need. |
| Relevant track | Systems, with a link to your reviewed home-server project. |
| Your level | Your current Systems level, so the team can check it against the request's requirements. |
| Why this machine | What you plan to run, how much memory it needs, and why the listed shop equipment doesn't suit it. |
| Delivery | Your country and any sourcing restrictions that affect the request. |

The team checks your progress, the item's purpose, cost and availability. If it can support the request, the quote states:

- The approved item and its base Ore price.
- The track level required and the level discount that applies.
- Whether a Systems Requisition is eligible and what it would save.
- The final Ore total and any delivery conditions.

You can accept if you meet the requirements and have enough Ore, or decline. This example describes the request process; it doesn't set a price or a level requirement for mini PCs. [Pricing](/docs/pricing#an-example) shows how an eligible discount is calculated.

## What the team looks at

A good request ties the item to a real build or a technical need you can show. The team also has to check that it can source, pay for and deliver the thing.

Meeting the level requirement lets you request an item. The team still needs to approve its cost and delivery.

## Requisitions and changes

A Requisition applies only if the final approved quote says it's eligible. The usual rules still hold: it has to match the field, meet the minimum value, and be the only one on the order.

If the specs or the supplier change, get a new quote before you treat the earlier one as final.

## Before you request

Check [program status](/docs/eligibility#where-things-stand) for request availability and the remaining tier and delivery decisions. Keep the approved quote: it records the item, requirements, final price and conditions you're accepting.
