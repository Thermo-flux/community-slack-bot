# What is LOADOUT?

Source: https://loadout-ysws-git-feature-all-changes-jerry-team1.vercel.app/docs/start

In development

LOADOUT is for teens aged 13 to 19 who want to build tools, servers, GPU projects or hardware. Ship your work for review and earn Ore for every approved hour. Spend it on equipment for your next build.

## Who it's for

You, if you're 13 to 19 and you like making things work. You don't need experience, only a project you want to build or fix.

There are four areas, which we call tracks: **Tools, Systems, Compute and Hardware**. A debugging tool counts. So does a sync engine, a custom renderer or a small instrument with a sensor. What matters is the technical part you built yourself, not what the finished thing is called.

## Your two Loadouts

| Loadout | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| Digital Loadout | Your accepted projects: the work you can show. |
| Physical Loadout | The gear you've bought with Ore for your next build. |

The loop is **build, write it up, ship it, get rated, earn Ore and XP, build again**. You can ship the same project more than once, and each ship pays for the new hours since the last one.

## What an hour is worth

A solid project earns **10 Ore for every approved hour**. Other builders rate your project from 0 to 20, and that rating is your rate: a rough but honest project pays about 5 an hour, a solid one 10, and an outstanding one can pay up to 20. Ten hours at a rating of 10 is 100 Ore.

Spend Ore on prizes and keep your Track XP as lasting progress. [Ore, XP & levels](/docs/progress) explains the awards and levels; [Prizes & requests](/docs/prizes) shows planned prices.

## How you join

You can start whenever you join. Ore and Track XP carry across seasons, so a builder who joins late keeps everything they earn from the day they start.

## Where things stand

LOADOUT is in development. [Current status and eligibility](/docs/eligibility#where-things-stand) has the launch status and the remaining decisions.

[RSVP](https://rsvp.soon.it/loadout) to receive launch updates. It records interest rather than enrolling you.

## Where to read next

Start with [what you can build](/docs/projects), then [the four tracks](/docs/tracks), then [what to hand in with a ship](/docs/shipping).

# What can I build?

Source: https://loadout-ysws-git-feature-all-changes-jerry-team1.vercel.app/docs/projects

[Program status →](/docs/eligibility#where-things-stand)

Build something that adds a useful technical capability. The product label doesn't decide whether it fits. The work inside it does.

## What makes a project fit?

Your submission should say:

- What technical capability you built or improved.
- What substantial work you did yourself.
- How someone else can inspect or check the result.
- Which track or tracks the work belongs to.

A bigger app can hold both real engineering and ordinary product work. Point to the technical core instead of claiming all of it.

## The four tracks

| Track | Typical technical core |
| --- | --- |
| Tools | Developer workflows, debugging, testing and automation. |
| Systems | Runtimes, protocols, storage and infrastructure. |
| Compute | Graphics, inference, numerical work and performance. |
| Hardware | Electronics, embedded systems, robotics and instruments. |

[The tracks page](/docs/tracks) helps you choose a field, including for projects that span several.

## Look for the technical core

| Shows little on its own | A stronger core to explain |
| --- | --- |
| A website copied from a tutorial. | An original tool that solves a real workflow problem. |
| A simple screen around an existing AI API. | A runtime or evaluation tool you designed and measured. |
| A standard product interface. | An original sync or rendering engine inside it. |

These are comparisons, not automatic yes or no answers. Reviewers still look at what's original, how deep it goes, how useful it is and what evidence you give.

## Research Mode

An experiment, benchmark, reproduction or investigation can fit even if it isn't a finished product. [Research Mode](/docs/research) explains the question, the method and the reproducible result it expects.

## Not sure your idea fits?

Say what capability you're building, what's original about it and how you'll show it works. Ask in **[#loadout-help](https://app.slack.com/client/E09V59WQY1E/C0C6XBLM0M7)** about fit, instead of guessing from a category name.

# Tracks

Source: https://loadout-ysws-git-feature-all-changes-jerry-team1.vercel.app/docs/tracks

[Program status →](/docs/eligibility#where-things-stand)

Pick the fields that describe your technical work, not just your project's label.

There are four tracks. Each is a field where your work can build up depth over time, and your progress in each one stays with you.

## The four fields

| Track | What you might build | What to show |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tools | A command-line tool, debugger, profiler, SDK, test system or developer automation. | How it makes a builder's work easier. |
| Systems | A runtime, a database component, a protocol, a deployment tool or a network service. | How it behaves, where it's limited and how reliable it is. |
| Compute | A renderer, an inference engine, a GPU workload, numerical code or an optimization. | That it's correct, with measurements under stated conditions. |
| Hardware | A PCB, an embedded device, part of a robot, an FPGA design or an instrument. | Why you designed it that way, and proof the physical result works. |

## One project, several tracks

Choose a main field and explain any extra technical work. Reviewers decide the final Track XP split from the work they approve.

Say a device has firmware, electronics and a profiling tool. That doesn't mean every team member gets the same split, and the same work can't earn several full awards.

## Why pick a track?

Track XP builds lasting progress in the fields you actually show. Higher levels in a field can lower prices on related prizes, and open some specialist prizes and Custom Order tiers.

[Ore, XP & levels](/docs/progress) explains how levels differ from Ore. [Pricing](/docs/pricing) explains how your specialty affects a purchase.

## Research is a mode

Research Mode can go with any of the four tracks. It isn't a fifth track, and it doesn't replace normal review.

# Research Mode

Source: https://loadout-ysws-git-feature-all-changes-jerry-team1.vercel.app/docs/research

[Program status →](/docs/eligibility#where-things-stand)

Research Mode is for investigating something technical inside Tools, Systems, Compute or Hardware. What you ship can be a finding someone else can reproduce, not a finished product.

## What makes it research?

Start with a question. Then answer it with an experiment, a benchmark, a reproduction or a measurement. Show what you did, what happened and what the result supports.

Looking into how a workload uses memory could fit Compute. Measuring how an instrument behaves could fit Hardware. The question and the work decide the track.

## What to include

- The question, the hypothesis, or the result you tried to reproduce.
- The method, the environment, the inputs and the versions that matter.
- Measurements and checks, including the attempts that failed.
- The code, designs or steps someone needs to repeat it.
- A conclusion that says what you couldn't rule out.

A graph without its method isn't enough. Keep what you measured separate from explanations you haven't tested.

## Negative results count if you explain them

An experiment that proves your idea wrong can teach someone a lot. Say why the result matters and show the evidence. An unfinished attempt isn't automatically an accepted research ship.

## Review and progress

Research goes through the same review as any ship: fit, validity, authorship and quality. It doesn't skip tracking, and it doesn't turn borrowed work into yours.

A reproducible report can earn extra Track XP. The bonus amount and its qualification details are still being settled; no numerical Research bonus is published. See [Build & ship](/docs/shipping) for evidence and [Project review](/docs/review) for the review stages.
